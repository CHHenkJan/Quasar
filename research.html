Foundations

The research behind Quasar.

The academic research and quantitative finance principles behind Quasar's adaptive methodology.

Three Pillars

Built on the foundations of modern quantitative finance.

Quasar's methodology draws from three areas of financial ML research. The academic research behind these methods is publicly available. Our specific implementation details are proprietary.

Feature Engineering & Signal Construction

Marcos López de Prado — "Advances in Financial Machine Learning"

López de Prado's meta-labeling framework provides a systematic approach to signal quality scoring. Rather than generating binary buy/sell signals, the framework first generates a primary signal, then uses a secondary model to assess the quality and confidence of that signal.

Quasar applies this concept through its confidence scoring system — each signal is evaluated for quality before emission, with only high-confidence signals reaching subscribers.

Regime Detection Methods

Ernest Chan & Stefan Jansen — "Machine Learning for Algorithmic Trading"

Regime detection is a well-established concept in quantitative finance. The core insight: markets alternate between trending and mean-reverting states, and strategies optimized for one state underperform in the other.

Quasar implements a simplified regime classification pipeline using ADX, Bollinger Band width, and momentum indicators — adapted for cryptocurrency market dynamics and the 4-hour timeframe.

Ensemble Methods for Trading

Yves Hilpisch — "Artificial Intelligence in Finance"

Ensemble methods combine multiple models or indicators to produce more robust predictions than any single model. In trading, this translates to multi-indicator systems where individual indicator failures are compensated by the ensemble.

Quasar's 5-indicator ensemble with dynamic weighting applies this principle — adjusting indicator influence based on the detected market regime rather than using fixed weights.

Implementation

From research to practice

Quasar's implementation of these academic concepts is adapted for cryptocurrency markets and runs on Cryptohopper's paper trading infrastructure. Several important distinctions between the academic research and our implementation:

  • Simplified models: Academic research often uses complex statistical methods (Hidden Markov Models, deep learning). Quasar uses interpretable indicators that can be verified and understood.
  • Crypto-specific adaptation: Academic research primarily covers traditional equity and forex markets. Cryptocurrency markets have unique characteristics (24/7 trading, higher volatility, different microstructure) that require adaptation.
  • Paper trading context: Our results are from paper trading, which does not account for real-world execution challenges like slippage, liquidity, and market impact.
  • No deep learning: The term "ML-enhanced" refers to multi-factor statistical models and adaptive weighting — not neural networks or deep learning systems.
