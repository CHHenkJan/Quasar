1. Paper Trading Disclosure

All trading signals, strategies, templates, and performance data presented on this website and through Quasar's Cryptohopper marketplace products are derived exclusively from paper trading (simulated trading) on the Cryptohopper platform.

Paper trading does not involve real capital. No real money is at risk in the generation of any results shown. Paper trading results do not account for:

  • Slippage (the difference between expected and actual execution price)
  • Liquidity constraints (inability to execute at desired price or size)
  • Market impact (the effect of your own orders on the market price)
  • Exchange downtime or connectivity issues
  • Fees and commissions (which vary by exchange and account type)

Real trading results will differ from paper trading results — potentially significantly. Paper trading performance should not be considered a reliable indicator of future real-trading performance.

2. Machine Learning Model Risk

Quasar uses statistical methods and indicators to classify market regimes and generate trading signals. The term "machine learning-enhanced" refers to the use of multi-factor statistical models and adaptive weighting — it does not imply that Quasar uses deep learning, neural networks, or any AI system that learns from real-time trading outcomes.

Specific ML-related risks include:

  • Regime misclassification: The model may incorrectly classify a trending market as ranging, or vice versa, leading to strategy misalignment and potential losses.
  • Overfitting risk: Statistical models calibrated on historical data may not perform as expected on future data. Historical patterns may not repeat.
  • Model degradation: Crypto markets evolve continuously. A model that performs well under current market conditions may not work in changed market structures.
  • Confidence score limitations: The confidence score reflects internal model agreement, not the probability of profitability. A high confidence score does not guarantee a profitable trade.

5. Adaptive Strategy Risk

Unlike fixed strategies, adaptive strategies change their behavior based on detected market conditions. This introduces unique risks that are not present in static trading systems:

  • Past performance in one market regime may not reflect performance in a different regime. Users should not extrapolate performance from trending market periods to ranging market periods or vice versa.
  • Regime transitions are the highest-risk periods for adaptive systems. During transitions, the system may briefly apply the wrong strategy configuration.
  • The adaptive mechanism itself may underperform a well-timed fixed strategy in certain market conditions.
  • Dynamic position sizing means risk exposure varies over time, making it harder to predict maximum potential loss.
